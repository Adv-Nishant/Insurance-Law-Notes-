Core Principles
The Nine Pillars

General Principles That Govern All Insurance

These principles apply universally — mastering them unlocks every specific type of insurance.

PILLAR 01
Uberrima Fides
Utmost Good Faith
The insurer knows nothing — the assured knows everything. Mutual duty of disclosure. Carter v. Boehm (1766).
PILLAR 02
Insurable Interest
Lucena v. Craufurd (1806)
Financial stake in the subject matter. Distinguishes insurance from wagering. Timing varies: Fire = policy & loss · Marine = loss only · Life = commencement only.
PILLAR 03
Indemnity
Castellain v. Preston (1883)
No profit from insurance. Restore insured to pre-loss position. Applies to Fire, Marine, Misc. Does NOT apply to Life (Dalby).
PILLAR 04
Causa Proxima
"Non remota spectatur"
Direct, dominant, operative cause. Not the remote cause. Applies universally. § 55 MIA 1963 codifies it for marine insurance.
PILLAR 05
Subrogation
§ 79 MIA 1963
Insurer steps into insured's shoes after payment. NOT applicable to Life. Limited to amount paid (Yorkshire v. N.S. Co.).
PILLAR 06
Contribution
§ 80 MIA 1963
Multiple insurers share loss proportionately. Insured cannot profit from double insurance. Formula: (Insurer's sum ÷ total) × loss.
PILLAR 07
Premium
§ 64VB Insurance Act
Consideration for risk. No risk assumed without advance premium. Days of grace typically 30 days. Forfeited on fraud.
PILLAR 08
Warranty
§ 35 MIA 1963
Must be exactly complied with — whether material or not. Breach = automatic discharge from date of breach. Irremedial (§ 36(2)).
PILLAR 09
Assignment
§ 38 Insurance Act
Life = assignable by endorsement. Marine = assignable before or after loss (§ 52 MIA). Fire = NOT automatically transferred. Rule in Dearle v. Hall — notice determines priority.
Key Statutes
Legislation

Governing Statutes & Their Scope

Know which statute governs which type — this alone can earn you marks in exams.

Insurance Act, 1938 (Act No. 4 of 1938)
The mother statute of Indian insurance law — enacted 26 Feb 1938, in force 1 Jul 1939. Governs registration of insurers, conduct of insurance business, investment, solvency, reinsurance (§§ 101A–101C). Still in force with multiple amendments.
Marine Insurance Act, 1963 (Act No. 11 of 1963)
Only dedicated statute for a specific type of insurance. 91 sections + Schedule. Codifies centuries of marine insurance common law. Based on English MIA 1906. Principles extended by analogy to fire and other general insurance.
IRDA Act, 1999 (Act No. 41 of 1999)
Constituted the IRDAI (Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India). Incorporated April 2000. Composition: 10 members (§ 4). Functions: § 14 — 17 functions including registration, policyholder protection, investment regulation.
LIC Act, 1956 (Act No. 31 of 1956)
Established the Life Insurance Corporation of India following nationalization ordinance of 19 Jan 1956. Key sections: § 4 (composition — max 16 members + Chairman) · § 5 (capital ₹5 crores) · § 6 (functions including best advantage of community).
Motor Vehicles Act, 1988
Sections 140–176 govern insurance and compensation. § 140: No-fault liability. § 146: Compulsory third-party insurance. § 149: Insurer must satisfy judgment. §§ 165–176: MACT. § 161: Solatium scheme for hit-and-run. Comprehensively amended in 2019.
Public Liability Insurance Act, 1991
Mandatory for owners handling hazardous substances. § 3: Strict no-fault liability — claimant need not prove negligence. Adjudicated by Collector. Schedule: Immediate relief amounts. § 7A: Environmental Relief Fund. Modelled on Rylands v. Fletcher + MC Mehta.
Leading Cases
Case Law Quick Reference

Must-Know Leading Cases

The cases every examiner expects you to know with facts, holding, and principle.

Carter v. Boehm (1766) — Lord Mansfield
Foundation of uberrima fides. Prudent insurer test established. Duty of disclosure is mutual.
Dalby v. India & London Assurance (1854)
Life insurance is NOT a contract of indemnity. Insurable interest required only at commencement.
Macaura v. Northern Assurance (1925)
Sole shareholder has no insurable interest in company's assets. Corporate identity is separate.
Castellain v. Preston (1883)
Indemnity principle in its purest form. Leading case on subrogation — insured cannot take from both hands.
LIC v. Shakuntala Devi
Trivial ailments (indigestion, headache) are not material facts. LIC held liable.
Harris v. Poland
Friendly fire causing loss to items not intended to be burnt = COVERED. Accidental = insured.
Symington v. Union Insurance
Acts to prevent fire spreading (cork thrown into sea) = covered as proximate fire loss.
Tootal v. London Fire Insurance
Earthquake = proximate cause of resulting fire. Fire insurer NOT liable.
Marsden v. City & Counties Insurance
Mob's lawlessness = independent novus actus interveniens. Fire insurer not liable for glass.
Asfar v. Blundell
Dates ceasing to be fit for human consumption = cease to be "thing of kind insured" = ATL.
Vacuum Oil v. Union Insurance
Prohibitive cost of forwarding damaged petroleum tins = Constructive Total Loss.
Thomas v. Hamilton (Inchmaree, 1891)
Valve damage not covered by standard marine perils → led to creation of the Inchmaree Clause.
Sarbati Devi v. Usha Devi (SC)
Nominee is "hand to receive" only — legal heirs retain rights. Modified by § 39(7)&(8) for close family.
Yorkshire Insurance v. Nisbet Shipping
Subrogation right is limited to the exact amount the insurer has paid — cannot recover more.
Dearle v. Hall
Priority among assignees of a life policy is determined by date of notice to the insurer, NOT date of assignment.
MC Mehta v. Union of India (1987)
Absolute liability for hazardous enterprises — beyond Rylands v. Fletcher, no exceptions. Article 21.
Swaran Singh (2004) 3 SCC 297
Fundamental vs. technical breach of motor policy. Framework for insurer's duty under § 149 MV Act.
Griffith v. Fleming
Insurable interest between spouses is presumed by law. Husband can claim on wife's life policy.
Beresford v. Royal Insurance (1938)
English law on suicide as felo de se. Lord Atkin — claim rejected. Indian law takes different position.
North British v. London & Liverpool (1877)
Leading case on contribution — pro-rata method established for double insurance.
Exam Strategy
How to Prepare

Examination Strategy & Tips

How to use these notes most effectively for your law examination.

📌
Section Numbers First
Every answer in a law examination should cite the relevant section number. Examiners award additional marks for precise statutory references. Know these by heart:
  • § 2(11), § 38, § 39, § 45 — Insurance Act 1938
  • §§ 19, 20, 34, 35, 57, 60, 79, 80 — MIA 1963
  • §§ 140, 146, 149, 165 — MV Act 1988
  • §§ 3, 4, 101A, 101B, 101C — Insurance Act
⚖
Case Law Format
When citing cases, follow this format: Case Name + Year + Key Principle. For leading cases, briefly state the facts, then the holding, then the principle. You don't need to cite all facts — the holding and principle are what matter most. For landmark cases like Dalby, Carter v. Boehm, Castellain v. Preston — know them perfectly.
🔍
The Critical Distinctions
Examiners love testing these contrasts:
  • Life vs. other insurance — indemnity / insurable interest timing
  • Warranty vs. Representation
  • Double Insurance vs. Reinsurance
  • ATL vs. CTL in marine
  • No-fault vs. Absolute Liability
  • Treaty vs. Facultative Reinsurance
📊
Use Formulas & Tables
For numerical topics, write the formula before the calculation. The Average Clause formula, Contribution formula, and CTL repair-cost test all follow a precise mathematical logic. Showing the formula earns marks even if you make an arithmetic error. Comparison tables also impress examiners in distinction-based questions.
🧵
Connect the Dots
Insurance law is one interconnected system. Subrogation flows from indemnity. Indemnity does not apply to life → therefore subrogation doesn't apply to life. Double insurance rules don't apply to life for the same reason. Reinsurance requires the cedant to have an insurable interest (§ 11(1) MIA). Weaving these connections into your answers shows mastery.
🎯
High-Priority Topics
Based on examination patterns, prioritize:
  • Uberrima Fides + Carter v. Boehm
  • Insurable Interest — all three types
  • Dalby — life insurance nature
  • Subrogation — Castellain v. Preston
  • CTL — § 60 MIA 1963
  • Double Insurance vs. Reinsurance
  • §§ 101A–101C reinsurance framework
